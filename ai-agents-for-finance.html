What We Build

Where Back-Office
Time Goes.

Most finance teams don't struggle with hard decisions. They struggle with volume: keying data, finding the matching document, and chasing approvals.

Invoice and PO intake

Agents read invoices as they arrive, extract the fields, check the vendor, and create the record.

Three-way matching

Invoices are compared to POs and receipts. Clean matches post. Mismatches queue with a plain note.

Approval routing

Each item goes to the right approver by your rules, with reminders and escalation when it stalls.

Reconciliation

Transactions are matched across bank and ledger data, with exceptions explained and ready for review.

Audit preparation

Documents, approvals, and notes are assembled as the work happens, so audit requests are quick.

Reporting packets

Recurring reports are drafted from your data on schedule for an analyst to review and send.

Example

Invoice day, handled.

  1. Invoices arrive in the shared inbox in six different formats.
  2. The agent extracts vendor, amounts, dates, and line items, and checks the vendor against your approved list.
  3. It finds the matching PO and compares quantities and prices. One invoice is $420 over.
  4. Clean invoices route for approval. The $420 exception goes to a buyer with a one-line explanation.
  5. Every step lands in an audit log: who, what, when, and why.

Control

Controls Stay Where They Belong.

  • Payment release. Your people approve payments above the limits you set. Agents prepare and route the work.
  • Segregation of duties. Agents follow the same separation your policies require.
  • Audit trail. Every action is logged in a form your auditors can read.
  • Least-privilege access. Each agent sees only the systems and data its job needs.
