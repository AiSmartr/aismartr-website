Speed to Lead for Real Estate: The AI Response Playbook

Most real estate leads get their first reply hours late, and the research says that is where deals get lost. Here's a playbook for answering in minutes, with the scripts and the handoff rules.

Short answer

Reply within 5 minutes, at any hour, with a message that asks one question and offers a showing time. The best-known study found the odds of reaching a lead fall 100 times between a 5-minute and a 30-minute response. Below are the numbers, the scripts, and the point where a person takes over.

How Fast Should a Real Estate Lead Get a Response?

Inside 5 minutes. The most-cited research is the InsideSales.com and MIT Lead Response Management Study, which tracked six companies, more than 15,000 leads, and over 100,000 call attempts across three years.

It found that the odds of contacting a lead drop 100 times when the first call comes at 30 minutes instead of 5. The odds of qualifying that lead drop 21 times.

A 2021 InsideSales analysis of 5.7 million inbound leads at more than 400 companies found the same pattern. Conversion rates were 8 times greater in the first five minutes, and only 0.1% of leads got a response that fast.

Harvard Business Review's 2011 study of online leads found the average company took 42 hours to respond, and nearly a quarter never responded at all.

How to read these numbers. The studies cover many industries and were run or funded by a lead-response vendor, and the 2007 data is about phone calls. Treat the exact multiples as direction, because the direction has held for years and your own CRM will show how it plays out in your market.

Why Do Leads Go Cold So Fast?

A lead who fills out a form at 9:47 p.m. is on their phone, interested, and looking at other listings. That moment of attention doesn't last.

The first reply that sounds like a person and offers a time usually gets the conversation. A reply the next morning arrives after the prospect has already talked to someone else.

Agents can't be available every hour. They're at showings, in closings, and asleep. That gap is the job for an AI response agent.

What Does an AI Response Agent Do in the First Five Minutes?

  1. Seconds 0 to 10. A form, portal inquiry, or missed call arrives. The agent logs the lead in your CRM and tags the source and listing.
  2. Seconds 10 to 60. It sends a text and an email that name the listing, say who it is, and ask one question.
  3. Minutes 1 to 3. It answers simple questions from your listing data (price, availability, HOA, open house times) and asks about timeline and budget.
  4. By minute 5. It offers two or three showing times pulled from the agent's real calendar, and books the one the lead picks.
  5. Anything else. It stops and hands the conversation to a person, with a short summary of what the lead said.

The agent works your lead sources, your CRM, and your calendar. It isn't a separate inbox to check.

What Should the First Message Say? Four Scripts

Every first message does four things: names the listing, says who is writing, asks one question, and gives an easy way to opt out of texts. Use these as starting points and put them in your own voice.

1. New web lead, daytime

Hi [Name], it's [Agent] at [Brokerage]. Thanks for asking about [Address]. Are you hoping to see it this week? I have [Day] at [time] or [Day] at [time]. Reply STOP to opt out.

2. New lead after hours

Hi [Name], thanks for reaching out about [Address] tonight. I'm the AI assistant for [Agent] at [Brokerage], and I can help right now. Are you looking to buy or rent, and when would you like to move? Reply STOP to opt out.

3. Open house follow-up, same day

Hi [Name], thanks for coming to [Address] today. What did you think of it? If you'd like a second look, or want to see similar homes, I can set up a time. Reply STOP to opt out.

4. Missed call text-back

Sorry we missed your call to [Brokerage]. I'm the AI assistant for [Agent]. What can I help with? I can answer questions about [Address] or book a showing. Reply STOP to opt out.

Tell people when they're talking to an assistant. It builds trust, and most leads care more about the fast, useful answer than who typed it.

Where Does a Person Take Over?

The agent handles speed, scheduling, and simple answers. A licensed agent handles judgment. Set these handoffs in writing before launch:

  • Pricing advice, offers, negotiation, and anything that sounds like a commitment
  • Questions about who lives in a neighborhood, or any request that touches a protected class (the agent must never steer or comment, and passes these to a person)
  • Any lead who asks for a human, sounds upset, or has a complicated situation
  • Any question the agent can't answer from your listing data

The Fair Housing Act prohibits discrimination in housing, and the agent's rules need to reflect that. Your broker or counsel should review the script set before it goes live.

What About Texting Consent?

Texting prospects is covered by the Telephone Consumer Protection Act. Collect clear consent on the form that creates the lead, honor opt-outs right away, and keep a record of the consent.

The rules around texting change, so have your counsel confirm the language on your forms.

How Do You Measure It?

Start with a baseline from your CRM for the last 30 days, then track the same five numbers every week:

  • Median time to first reply
  • Share of leads answered in under 5 minutes
  • Contact rate (leads who replied or picked up)
  • Showings booked per 100 leads
  • Leads that become clients

One free test you can run today: submit a lead through your own website at 9:47 p.m. on a Friday and time the reply. If you can't beat 5 minutes, you've found the first thing to fix.
