AP Automation for Small Accounting Firms: Software or Custom Agent?

Accounts payable runs on intake, matching, and approvals, and a small firm has two ways to automate it. Here's how to choose between AP software and a custom agent, with the numbers and the controls.

Short answer

Buy AP software when your invoices, approvals, and accounting system fit its standard workflow, and build a custom agent when your rules, clients, or systems don't. Many small firms do both: software for the common path, an agent for the exceptions. Pilot on three months of old invoices before you trust either.

This applies to a firm's own payables and to the AP work an accounting or bookkeeping practice runs for clients. The same decision comes up in both.

What Does AP Cost Per Invoice, and Where Does the Time Go?

Ardent Partners' 2026 benchmark puts the average cost to process one invoice at $9.90. Best-in-Class teams (the 20% with the lowest cost and shortest cycle times) process one for $2.67. Nearly 1 in 5 invoices hits an exception, and Best-in-Class teams hold that to 11.8%.

What Does AP Software Do Well?

AP software is the category with products like Bill, Ramp, Dext, and Stampli. We name them for orientation only, with no ranking or endorsement.

The category handles the common path well:

  • Capturing invoices from email and reading the fields
  • Coding to your chart of accounts
  • Routing approvals by amount or department
  • Paying vendors and syncing to your accounting system

It's fast to start, the vendor maintains it, and the upfront cost is low. For a firm with one entity and a standard approval chain, it's often the right answer.

Where Does AP Software Run Out of Road?

Software is built for the standard workflow, so friction starts where your work isn't standard:

  • Many clients, different rules. Each client has its own coding rules, approvers, and thresholds, and each one needs its own setup.
  • Messy inputs. Scans, photos, and one-off formats still land in an exception queue.
  • Matching beyond the basics. Checking an invoice against a PO, a receipt, and a contract term takes logic the product may not offer.
  • Unusual systems. If your ledger or approval tool has no clean integration, people re-key data by hand.
  • Exceptions. Software flags the problem. A person still researches it, drafts the fix, and chases the answer.

What Does a Custom AP Agent Do?

An agent does the work around the invoice as well as the keying. A typical build reads each invoice and checks the vendor against your master list. It matches the PO, codes the invoice using each client's rules, and routes it for approval.

On an exception, it works out what's wrong. It might be an invoice $420 over its PO or a new bank account on a known vendor. The agent sends a person a one-line note with the evidence attached, and logs every step for the auditor.

An agent can also work inside the software you already run, the way an employee would. We cover that in agents that use your software.

Choose software if your approval chain is simple, your invoices are mostly clean, and your accounting system has a supported integration.

Choose a custom agent if you run AP for many clients with different rules, your exception queue eats real hours, or your systems don't connect cleanly.

Choose both if software handles the clean 80% and an agent works the exceptions and the cross-system matching. This is the setup we'd test first for most small firms.

What Controls Should Any AP Automation Have?

Whichever route you pick, these controls stay the same:

  • Separation of duties. The automation never approves and releases the same payment on its own.
  • Approval thresholds. Anything above a set amount goes to a person.
  • Vendor change checks. A new bank account on a known vendor triggers a call-back to a number you already have on file. The FBI's 2024 Internet Crime Report lists business email compromise at $2.77 billion in reported losses.
  • Duplicate detection. Same vendor, same amount, same week gets flagged.
  • A complete log. Every read, match, and routing decision is recorded and reviewable.
  • Least-privilege access. The automation sees only the ledgers and clients it needs.

How Do You Test Before You Trust It?

  1. Shadow mode. Run the tool on the last three months of invoices without paying anything. Compare its coding, matching, and flags to what your team actually did.
  2. Measure. Track match accuracy, exception rate, and time per invoice against your own baseline and the benchmarks above.
  3. Supervised live. Let it work real invoices with a person approving every one for a few weeks.
  4. Widen slowly. Raise the auto-route limits only for categories that stay clean.
